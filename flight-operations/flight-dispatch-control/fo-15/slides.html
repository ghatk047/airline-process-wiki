1.1 Receive Shipper DG Declaration & Air Waybill DG acceptance processing time ≤30 min per shipment Incomplete or incorrectly completed shipper declarations are the leading cause of DG non-compliance; IATA DGR Sec 8.1.6 mandates specific formats but non-industry shippers routinely submit non-conforming documents requiring full re-inspection
1.2 Validate UN Number and Proper Shipping Name Classification error rate <0.5% of DG shipments processed IATA DGR is revised annually; local DGR lookup databases at handling agents may lag the official publication by 4–8 weeks, causing classification mismatches particularly for newly added UN numbers in Class 9 ⚠
1.3 Verify Packing Group, Quantity Limits & Label Compliance DG package rejection rate for non-compliance ≤2% of DG shipments; lithium battery state-of-charge verification rate 100% Lithium battery shipments (Class 9, UN3480/3481) account for >40% of DG non-compliance findings; IATA DGR Sec 3.9 requires state-of-charge ≤30% which is physically unverifiable without specialised equipment, creating acceptance risk ⚠
1.4 Check Operator Acceptance Policy & Forbidden DG List Operator policy compliance rate 100%; forbidden item interception rate 100% Individual airlines maintain forbidden DG lists that exceed IATA minimums; policy updates are not always propagated to the CargoWise rules engine in real time, creating a window where inadvertent acceptance of operator-prohibited DG is possible ⚠
2.1 Verify Aircraft Type DG Limits (PAX vs CAO) Per-aircraft DG limit compliance rate 100%; last-minute aircraft swap DG re-check completion rate 100% Mixed fleet operations mean PAX aircraft quantity limits are significantly more restrictive than CAO limits for the same DG class; last-minute aircraft swaps change applicable limits and require full re-validation — Altéa DCS does not automatically trigger re-check on tail number change ⚠
2.2 Validate State & Overflight DG Restrictions for Routing State restriction check completion rate 100% per DG-carrying flight; overflight violation rate 0% IATA DGR Addendum for State Variations is updated bi-annually but some states (notably China CAAC and several Middle East CAAs) impose interim restrictions not captured in the published addendum, requiring manual dispatcher lookup against state CAA circulars ⚠
2.3 Confirm Operator Approval for Restricted DG Classes Approval verification cycle time ≤4 hours for restricted classes; approval expiry miss rate 0% Class 7 radioactive material requires Transport Index calculations and specific CAA approvals that expire; renewal tracking at most carriers relies on manual spreadsheets with no system-enforced expiry alert in CargoWise, creating regulatory exposure under IATA DGR Sec 10.8 ⚠
3.1 Assign ULD Position per DG Segregation Rules DG stowage compliance rate 100%; CG deviation ≤±0.5% MAC from target Altéa LoadPlanner's DG segregation module requires manual overrides when multiple incompatible DG classes are present on the same flight; there is no automated incompatibility alert in the base LoadPlanner configuration, relying entirely on load controller knowledge
3.2 Run DG Compatibility Matrix Check Across All Shipments Segregation non-conformance rate 0%; compatibility check cycle time ≤10 min IATA DGR Table 9.3 covers 49 hazard class combinations; Altéa LoadPlanner automates checks for only 12 primary class pairs, requiring manual cross-reference for mixed-class loads — a high-error-risk step during high-density cargo operations ⚠
3.3 Calculate Total DG Quantities vs Aircraft Hold Limits Per-hold DG limit compliance 100%; NEM calculation accuracy within ±1% Cumulative DG quantity limits per hold require aggregating across multiple individual shipments; there is no automated per-hold summation in standard Altéa LoadPlanner, exposing load controllers to arithmetic error risk on high-volume DG flights ⚠
3.4 Generate NOTOC Draft in IATA Standard Format NOTOC generation time ≤15 min after load plan finalised; NOTOC accuracy rate ≥99.9% NOTOC format requirements vary by state CAA — some authorities (FAA, EASA) require additional supplementary fields beyond the IATA standard template; Altéa NOTOC templates are not always maintained to reflect the latest state-specific variants, requiring manual addenda
4.1 Dispatcher DG Load Authorization Sign-Off Authorization completion ≥D-45 min (45 min before departure); 100% sign-off completion rate ICAO Annex 18 and 49 CFR Part 172 require dispatchers to hold current DG recurrent training (every 24 months); expired training certificates are not always flagged in Altéa DCS against the authorising dispatcher's profile, creating a silent regulatory non-compliance risk ⚠
4.2 Transmit NOTOC to Captain via ACARS Datalink NOTOC ACARS delivery confirmation rate ≥99%; delivery completed ≥D-30 min ACARS uplink to an aircraft on the ground can fail when avionics are in pre-power state during early turnaround; backup paper NOTOC delivery via physical runner to the gate adds 15–20 min and relies on ramp coordination that frequently breaks down at congested hubs ⚠
4.3 Captain Reviews and Accepts NOTOC Captain NOTOC rejection rate <1% of DG flights; acceptance completed ≥D-20 min Under ICAO Annex 18 and FAR 121.285 the captain has absolute authority to offload DG; rejections after DG is physically loaded require ramp return and cause an average 35-min departure delay — root cause is often insufficient pre-departure DG briefing to the cockpit crew ⚠
5.1 Ramp Agent Loads DG per NOTOC Stowage Instructions DG loading position accuracy 100%; loading completion ≥D-25 min Ramp teams handle DG orientation labels and class placards under intense time pressure; IATA DGR Sec 9.3 requires 'This Way Up' orientation labels on liquid DG — compliance miss rates spike during peak turn operations when the same ramp team handles multiple aircraft simultaneously
5.2 Verify DG Load Position and Condition Post-Loading DG load verification completion rate 100% ≥D-20 min; discrepancy rate <0.5% Hold access for DG load verification requires DG-certified personnel per IATA DGR Sec 9.4; at out-stations with thin staffing the verification is sometimes conducted by non-certified ramp agents, constituting a regulatory non-compliance that is rarely detected until a line audit ⚠
5.3 Update Final Loadsheet with Confirmed DG Positions Loadsheet finalisation ≥D-15 min; weight discrepancy vs actual ≤0 kg Late DG offloads — triggered by captain rejection or discovered package damage — require full loadsheet reissue; Altéa DCS reissue and re-signature cycle averages 8–12 min, severely compressing the pre-departure critical path and contributing to D0 departure misses
6.1 Transmit DG Manifest to Destination & En Route Stations Destination DG advance notification lead time ≥2 hours before scheduled arrival (IATA RP 1654); transmission success rate 100% IATA RP 1654 requires advance DG notification to the destination station; system-to-system messaging reliability degrades at stations running legacy DCS versions incompatible with modern SITA XML message formats, requiring manual telephone or email follow-up that is frequently missed
6.2 File DG Occurrence Report if Incident Detected DG incident reporting compliance 100%; report filed ≤72 hours of incident per ICAO Annex 18 DG incident under-reporting is a recognised industry-wide issue; ramp and cargo staff are reluctant to self-report fearing personal liability — airlines that have not established non-punitive SMS reporting cultures consistently show under-reporting rates estimated at 60–80% of actual DG incidents ⚠
6.3 Archive DG Documentation per Regulatory Retention Policy Documentation archival rate 100% within 4 hours of flight departure; retrieval time for audit requests ≤2 min IATA DGR mandates minimum 3-month retention while FAA 49 CFR Part 175 requires 2 years and some EASA states require longer; carriers operating across jurisdictions must apply the longest applicable period globally, but CargoWise retention defaults are often configured to the shortest period, creating audit exposure
