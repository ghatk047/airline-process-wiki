StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Receive OFP computation trigger from schedule OFP computation initiated ≥3 hrs before STD; 100% of flights have confirmed OFP before T-90 min Late aircraft swaps or gate changes in Altéa trigger cascading OFP recomputations; absence of real-time webhook integration between Altéa and NAVBLUE forces manual re-triggers, creating a 10–20 min dispatcher workload spike per swap event
1.2 Extract aircraft type, reg, and ZFW estimate Preliminary ZFW within ±2% of final ZFW to ensure accurate early fuel computation Early-trigger OFPs (T-12 hr) carry highly uncertain load estimates; late check-in surges force ZFW and fuel recomputation within the T-1 hr window, causing ramp delays and re-fuelling events that average 18 min delay per occurrence
1.3 Pull upper-wind, weather, and SIGMET data Weather data refresh cycle ≤1 hr before STD; SIGMET coverage 100% of planned route corridor GoDirect data ingestion latency vs. real-time MWO (Meteorological Watch Office) SIGMET issuance can leave a 30–45 min gap for fast-moving convective systems; legacy dispatch integrations without automated SIGMET push alerts rely on dispatcher manual polling
2.1 Compute CI-optimised route — NAVBLUE Fuel saving vs. fixed routing ≥1.2% per flight; cost index adherence to fleet policy (CI 20–35 narrow-body); computation time ≤90 sec Aircraft performance model currency is a persistent risk — NAVBLUE performance database must be synced with aircraft modification records from AMOS; unrecorded winglet upgrades or engine modifications cause model accuracy drift of up to 0.8% fuel burn, skewing fleet-wide efficiency analytics
2.2 Calculate trip, contingency, alternate, and reserve fuel Actual vs. planned fuel burn variance ≤±3%; zero-fuel-on-arrival (ZFOA) rate <0.1% of flights FAR Part 91/121 domestic alternate fuel rules differ from ICAO Annex 6 international rules; carriers operating trans-border routes must maintain dual rule-sets in NAVBLUE configuration — version drift during software updates causes compliance risk and has triggered FAA Letter of Investigation (LOI) actions at US carriers
2.3 Validate fuel order within MTOW and structural limits MTOW exceedance rate 0% (hard regulatory limit); fuel reduction iteration cycles ≤2 per flight before escalation High summer temperatures (ISA+15°C or above) at hot/high airports (PHX, DEN, LAS) significantly reduce MTOW-limited payload; NAVBLUE does not provide automated MTOW-constrained route re-optimisation — dispatchers perform manual iteration, averaging 4 additional minutes per constrained flight ⚠
3.1 Filter and review NOTAMs for route and aerodromes Dispatcher NOTAM review time ≤8 min per flight; Type I/II NOTAM inclusion rate 100% NOTAM data quality is a persistent industry problem — free-text, unstructured NOTAMs from certain states require manual parsing; Jeppesen Smart NOTAM keyword filtering reduces volume but has suppressed safety-critical items when taxonomy files are not updated after ICAO format revisions (ICAO Doc 8126 Annex 15 transition 2023)
3.2 Check ATFM flow programmes and special use airspace ATFM programme impact assessment completed ≥2 hr before STD; SUA conflict detection rate 100% FAA TFMS GDP data is refreshed every 15–30 min; dispatchers using static NAVBLUE routing miss late-issued GDPs and incur unplanned EDCT delays at departure; military SUA activation in the Gulf of Mexico MOA complex is issued with <1 hr notice, invalidating already-filed ATC FPLs
3.3 Validate route is clear of restrictions and ATFM conflicts Reroute rate due to late SUA/flow conflicts <2% of flights; reroute cycle time (detection to new FPL) ≤12 min Military SUA activation notices (NOTAMs for ranges like W-151, W-168 Gulf ops) can arrive within 45 min of ETD, requiring an emergency reroute that bypasses normal NAVBLUE optimisation and may add 80–150 kg fuel burn on affected sectors ⚠
4.1 File ICAO FPL with ATC via SITA AFTN datalink FPL filed ≥60 min before STD (FAR 91.169 / ICAO Annex 2 requirement); FPL acceptance rate (ACK without CHG) ≥94% SITA AFTN message queuing during peak traffic windows (0600–0900 hub banks) adds 5–12 min filing latency; rejected FPLs (CHG messages) require manual field correction and re-filing, consuming 8–15 min of dispatcher time and compressing the pre-departure preparation window
4.2 Assess EDCT/CTOT slot assignment from ATFM authority EDCT compliance rate ≥98%; average ATFM delay per affected flight <8 min Multiple overlapping GDPs (destination GDP + en-route AFP simultaneously) create conflicting EDCT windows that TFMS cannot always auto-resolve; dispatchers must manually arbitrate priority — a process with no formal FAA guidance, leading to inconsistent outcomes across carriers ⚠
4.3 Negotiate slot relief or reroute via AOC coordination Slot relief request success rate ≥30% of attempts; crew duty limit infringement due to ATFM delay <0.5% of flights FSM slot-swap portal requires AOC login and a dedicated coordinator — carriers without 24/7 AOC staffing (regional partners, red-eye operations) accept EDCT passively and absorb avoidable delays averaging 22 min per event
5.1 Generate and transmit OFP package to crew EFB OFP delivery to EFB ≥T-60 min before STD; datalink transmission success rate ≥99.5% NAVBLUE and Jeppesen FliteDeck EFB require matching document schema versions; version mismatches between aircraft EFB software (often on a slower certification update cycle) and NAVBLUE OFP output format cause rendering failures — crews revert to printed paper fallback, adding 10–15 min to pre-departure preparation
5.2 Captain reviews OFP fuel, route, and alternates on EFB Captain OFP review time ≤15 min; OFP acceptance without fuel change ≥82% of flights Captains on specific high-variability route segments (mountain wave, oceanic NAT) routinely add conservative discretionary fuel above company minima due to low confidence in NAVBLUE model accuracy on those segments — creates 0.3–0.8% fuel burn inefficiency per sector and can push TOW toward MTOW limits on weight-critical flights
5.3 Resolve captain commander's discretion fuel request Commander's discretion fuel (CDF) additions >200 kg rate <18% of flights; MTOW exceedance from CDF uplift 0% No structured data capture of CDF rationale in NAVBLUE or FliteDeck — inability to retrospectively analyse CDF patterns by route, captain, or weather condition prevents fuel policy refinement and limits regulatory evidence-gathering under ICAO Annex 6 / EU-OPS ORO.MLR.101 fuel documentation requirements ⚠
6.1 Monitor en-route fuel burn via ACARS position reports Fuel monitoring update interval ≤15 min oceanic / ≤5 min domestic; fuel deviation alert threshold ±5% of plan ADS-C oceanic position reports over HF/SATCOM on North Atlantic and Pacific tracks have 10–30 min latency; dispatchers cannot confirm fuel state in real time for ETOPS critical fuel scenarios and rely on crew self-reporting — a single-point-of-failure monitoring model that increases diversion initiation latency when fuel deviation occurs
6.2 Assess fuel deviation and issue diversion recommendation Fuel emergency declaration rate (MAYDAY FUEL / PAN PAN FUEL) <0.01% of flights; diversion for fuel exhaustion <0.05% of flights Dispatcher authority to recommend diversion is legally advisory-only under FAR 121.533; PIC has final authority, but late-stage diversions after dispatcher flags critical fuel state incur $40K–$120K IROPS cost per event — absence of a shared decision-making protocol between crew and dispatcher increases initiation latency by an average of 8 min ⚠
6.3 Close flight plan and record actuals in data lake Flight plan closure within 30 min of ATA (ICAO Doc 4444 requirement); fuel burn data capture rate 100% for post-flight analytics NAVBLUE actuals data is not natively streamed to the airline data lake; a manual export or scheduled ETL pipeline introduces 4–12 hr latency before actuals reach AWS Redshift — preventing same-day fuel efficiency feedback loops and delaying monthly CORSIA fuel reporting submissions
