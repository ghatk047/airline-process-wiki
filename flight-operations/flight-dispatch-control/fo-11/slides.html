1.1 Verify ETOPS route approval in OpSpecs 100% of ETOPS-routed flights pre-validated against OpSpecs before release OpSpec revisions under FAR 121.161 / ICAO Doc 10085 require 30-day authority lead time; last-minute network re-routing post-approval is operationally prohibited and forces costly re-planning ⚠
1.2 Confirm aircraft ETOPS certification level Zero ETOPS departures without valid airworthiness certificate and ETOPS approval on record ETOPS certification can be suspended mid-rotation due to maintenance findings; AMOS aircraft status must be queried in real time — stale cache data in dispatch systems has caused near-misses on ETOPS eligibility checks
2.1 Retrieve ETOPS alternate airport weather forecasts Weather data currency ≤1 hour before departure; TAF coverage ≥18 hours ahead of ETP crossing Remote oceanic alternates (e.g., NFFN Nadi, PGUM Guam, BIKF Keflavik) have sparse observation networks; TAF accuracy degrades beyond 12 hours, leaving critical ETP windows with low-confidence forecasts
2.2 Validate ETOPS alternate airport operational status 100% alternate validation completed ≥2 hours before scheduled departure NOTAM closure of a primary alternate (e.g., runway works at Honolulu's divert field) forces complete replanning; NOTAM parsing tools remain largely manual, and auto-filtering tools miss complex conditional NOTAMs issued by some Pacific island states ⚠
2.3 Calculate Equal Time Point and Point of No Return ETP calculation within ±2 nm of independent manual cross-check; PNR fuel state verified against aircraft performance tables NAVBLUE wind model integration updates every 6 hours; stale wind data on long trans-oceanic sectors (>14 hr block) can shift ETP by up to 40 nm, materially affecting alternate fuel requirements and crew decision windows
3.1 Review ETOPS significant systems maintenance status ETOPS maintenance check completed ≥90 min before scheduled departure; zero open ETOPS-critical MEL items at release ETOPS MEL (Minimum Equipment List) items differ substantively from standard MEL — APU, dual hydraulics, FADEC, SATCOM, and fire suppression must all be assessed against ETOPS-specific criteria; staff confusion on MMEL Part B boundaries generates repeated compliance queries and delays ⚠
3.2 Obtain dual maintenance and operations ETOPS release 100% of ETOPS flights have both maintenance certification and dispatcher release before pushback Paper tech log workflows at outstations create risk of missing or illegible ETOPS-specific entries; digital tech log adoption is incomplete, and remote stations lack AMOS real-time connectivity, causing release delays of 20–45 min
4.1 Calculate ETOPS fuel requirements ETOPS fuel uplift within ±2% of planned quantity; zero short-fuel departures ETOPS fuel requirements stack on top of standard dispatch reserves; on maximum-range sectors the cumulative fuel obligation can impose a 3–5 tonne payload penalty, directly reducing revenue yield on high-demand routes
4.2 Validate ETOPS fuel load against MTOW constraints Zero overweight departures; fuel optimised within 500 kg of minimum legal ETOPS load On peak summer rotations, ETOPS fuel combined with full passenger payload frequently pushes narrow-body aircraft to MTOW; payload trimming displaces revenue passengers and triggers downstream compensation and rebooking costs ⚠
4.3 File ETOPS-annotated ATC flight plan via AFTN Flight plan filed and acknowledged ≥60 min before scheduled departure; zero unacknowledged plans at departure ICAO Item 18 TBE (ETOPS) field is not consistently parsed by all regional ATC automation systems; Pacific RVSM and North Atlantic Track coordination require secondary manual validation with Shanwick / Oakland Oceanic, adding 15–30 min to filing cycle
5.1 Monitor ETOPS significant systems via ACARS / SATCOM ACARS position report receipt ≤15 min intervals on oceanic tracks; 100% engine health message coverage HF radio dependency in ACARS dead zones (South Pacific, polar routes beyond SATCOM footprint) creates monitoring gaps of 30–60 min; aircraft without Swift Broadband (SBB) upgrade rely on VHF-only ACARS, which drops out on oceanic tracks
5.2 Update ETOPS alternate weather at ETP crossings Weather reassessment delivered to flight crew ≤30 min before each ETP; 100% ETP coverage on all ETOPS sectors Rapidly developing convective weather at oceanic alternates (e.g., Bermuda in Atlantic hurricane season, Midway Atoll in typhoon season) can invalidate ETOPS release post-departure; regulatory requirement to notify crew and assess divert feasibility creates a compressed decision window once the PNR is passed ⚠
5.3 Assess engine or system anomaly for divert decision Divert decision communicated within ≤15 min of anomaly detection; ATC re-clearance obtained within ≤20 min Dual authority model (dispatcher + PIC) under FAR 121 for ETOPS divert decisions creates communication latency; SATCOM voice quality on older long-haul narrow-body fleets is often insufficient for complex technical discussions, forcing dispatcher to rely on ACARS text exchange with limited bandwidth ⚠
6.1 Complete ETOPS post-flight operational report Report filed within 24 hours of landing; 100% compliance rate on regulatory audit cycle Manual aggregation of ACARS logs, crew debrief, and maintenance notes creates a 4–6 hour compilation burden per ETOPS flight; no automated data ingestion pipeline exists in most legacy ops platforms, making timely filing dependent on individual dispatcher diligence
6.2 Update ETOPS reliability programme and IFSD database In-Flight Shutdown (IFSD) rate ≤0.02 per 1,000 engine hours (ETOPS-180 regulatory threshold); reliability report issued within 30 days of each reporting period IFSD rate trending requires cross-fleet data aggregation over a 12-month minimum window; smaller operators and new ETOPS entrants fall below the statistical significance threshold required by authorities for self-approval, creating dependency on OEM data sharing and authority-imposed restrictions ⚠
