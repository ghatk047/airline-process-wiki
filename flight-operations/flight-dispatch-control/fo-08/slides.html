StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Monitor real-time flight progress network-wide Flight tracking refresh rate ≤60 sec; network visibility ≥99.5% uptime ACARS datalink gaps over oceanic/remote sectors create blind spots; controller must cross-reference multiple screens to build situational awareness
1.2 Ingest weather and ATC flow constraint data Weather advisory ingestion latency ≤5 min from SIGMET issue FAA TFMS GDP notifications arrive via multiple channels (NAS Status, SWIM API, phone) with no single consolidated feed — manual reconciliation required
1.3 Assess airport capacity and slot utilisation Gate conflict identification ≥30 min before scheduled pushback At slot-controlled airports (LHR, JFK), late slot returns trigger IATA WASG 80:20 use-it-or-lose-it penalties — NOC must balance recovery options against slot forfeiture risk
1.4 Detect operational deviation and trigger disruption alert Alert-to-triage response time ≤10 min for Severity 1 events Cairo AI cascade modelling accuracy degrades when multiple simultaneous disruptions interact — controller must validate AI-generated impact scores manually for compounding events ⚠
2.1 Classify disruption severity and operational scope Correct severity classification rate ≥95% vs post-event review No standardised severity matrix shared between NOC, Crew Scheduling, and Maintenance Control — classification disagreements delay activation of the correct recovery protocol ⚠
2.2 Model downstream cascade impact on network Cascade model produced within 5 min of S1/S2 alert; PAX-at-risk count accuracy ±5% Aircraft tail swap history held in Jeppesen Crew and SITA AMS separately — reconciling rotational conflicts across systems requires manual data export and is error-prone at speed
2.3 Escalate network-wide crisis to senior NOC leadership Senior leadership notified within 15 min of S1 classification Airline-specific: post-Dec 2022 IROPS crisis, several carriers lack documented escalation runbooks — NOC managers default to ad hoc phone calls, creating coordination gaps during high-stress events ⚠
3.1 Identify available spare aircraft for recovery Spare aircraft identified within 20 min of disruption classification for S1/S2 events Unscheduled AOG maintenance removes spare aircraft without real-time update to NOC fleet board — controllers discover unavailability only when attempting to assign tail, causing recovery plan rework
3.2 Model and score recovery scenario options ≥3 recovery scenarios generated per S1/S2 event; scenario scoring completed within 10 min Cairo AI optimisation is constrained by static crew legality rules embedded at implementation — dynamic FAR Part 117 duty time calculations for mixed international/domestic pairings require manual override by Crew Scheduling ⚠
3.3 Evaluate crew duty legality for recovery assignments Zero FAR Part 117 duty-time violations in executed recovery plan; crew legality check completed within 15 min Jeppesen legality engine checks planned duties but cannot pre-validate real-time operator-initiated tail swaps — illegal assignments may only surface at sign-in, causing last-minute scrambles ⚠
3.4 Select and approve final recovery plan Recovery plan approved and published within 30 min of S1 disruption onset Financial authority thresholds for ferry flights and hotel costs are not pre-delegated to NOC duty managers — approvals requiring VP sign-off during off-peak hours introduce 30–60 min delays ⚠
4.1 Issue crew reassignment instructions to Crew Scheduling Crew contact-and-confirm cycle completed within 20 min; reserve activation rate ≥90% first contact Crew contactability drops during off-hours — contactability SLA is not contractually mandated beyond 2-hour reserve callout window, limiting rapid IROPS response capability ⚠
4.2 Coordinate aircraft maintenance release with MCC Aircraft release decision issued within 30 min of MCC request for non-AOG aircraft MEL deferral decisions on borderline items require OEM (Boeing) consultation — Boeing's 24/7 technical operations line response time can reach 60+ min for complex MEL calls, blocking aircraft release ⚠
4.3 Issue gate changes and ground resource reallocation Ground resources confirmed at revised gate ≥45 min before revised departure At contracted-handler airports, gate change notifications must be transmitted via SITA messaging — handlers operating legacy systems acknowledge with 15–30 min lag, preventing NOC from confirming readiness
5.1 Update revised schedule and flight status in Altéa PSS Schedule update published to GDS within 10 min of plan approval; zero discrepancies between Altéa and SITA AMS flight status Manual rescheduling in Altéa for >10 flights simultaneously requires sequential data entry — bulk SSIM import is available but requires IT ops involvement, creating a bottleneck during mass IROPS events
5.2 Trigger automated passenger rebooking workflow ≥85% of disrupted PAX rebooked within 2 hours of cancellation; EU261/DOT delay notification compliance 100% Altéa automated rebooking applies FIFO logic that ignores high-value loyalty tier passengers — premium PAX may be rebooked on worse itineraries than low-tier PAX unless NDC/API rules override is manually triggered ⚠
5.3 Monitor recovery execution and track plan adherence Recovery plan adherence ≥80% of revised departure times met within ±15 min; total network delay minutes reduced ≥40% vs no-action baseline No unified real-time recovery adherence score exists — NOC manager must manually correlate Altéa flight status, Jeppesen crew sign-ins, and SITA AMS gate data to determine if recovery is on track ⚠
6.1 Document NOC event log and decision audit trail Event documentation completed within 4 hours of network recovery; 100% of S1 events produce a formal After-Action Report Decision rationale during fast-moving IROPS is rarely captured in real time — post-event reconstruction from chat logs and ACARS data is time-consuming and incomplete, undermining root cause analysis
