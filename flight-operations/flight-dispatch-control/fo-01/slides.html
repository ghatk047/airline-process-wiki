StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Retrieve active flight plan from flight planning system Flight plan available ≥60 min before STD; plan accuracy within ±2% of actual fuel burn NAVBLUE integration latency with ATC slot data can delay plan finalization for constrained airspace; manual re-route required for TFR-affected corridors
1.2 Pull METAR, TAF, SIGMET and PIREP weather data Weather package delivered ≤45 min before STD; SIGMET currency ≤6 hrs SIGMETs for en-route convection update every 4–6 hrs; late-breaking convective SIGMETs may not be captured before release window, requiring mid-dispatch reassessment
1.3 Assess weather at origin, destination and alternate Destination forecast visibility and ceiling at or above published CAT I/II/III minima at ETA Rapid deterioration below CAT II minima at destination with simultaneous weather impact on primary alternate forces late alternate change, disrupting fuel calculation and ATC filing ⚠
1.4 Calculate fuel uplift including contingency and alternate Planned fuel ≥ FAR 121.645 / ICAO Annex 6 minimum (trip + 5% contingency + alternate + 30 min final reserve); fuel overage ≤3% of trip fuel EDCT ground delay burn estimates from FAA TFMS are systematically optimistic; dispatcher must manually add 10–15% buffer for GDP-affected departures, yet excess uplift increases fuel cost by ~$8/kg ⚠
2.1 File IFR flight plan with ATC via AFTN/SITA Flight plan filed ≥30 min before EOBT; filing acknowledgment received ≤5 min EUROCONTROL NM slot allocation (CTOT) can force departure time change ≤2 hrs before departure, requiring immediate re-file and downstream fuel/crew duty recalculation
2.2 Confirm ATC slot/EDCT and assess crew duty impact EDCT compliance rate ≥98%; crew duty limit breach rate <0.5% of dispatched flights Ground Delay Programs (GDPs) can shift EDCT by 60–180 min, pushing ATD past crew rest trigger thresholds under FAR Part 117; late crew swap triggers cascading downstream cancellations ⚠
2.3 Select and file alternate aerodrome with fuel coverage Alternate meets FAR 121.619 one-hour TAF forecast above alternate minima at ETA ±1 hr Popular alternates (e.g. ORD when MDW is destination) can become simultaneously weather-affected; dispatcher must identify second-order alternates increasing fuel requirement and potentially triggering MTOW constraint ⚠
3.1 Check aircraft airworthiness status in MRO system Aircraft status confirmed ≥90 min before STD; zero open airworthiness directives beyond MEL tolerance Late squawks logged by inbound crew within 45 min of departure may not propagate to AMOS before the dispatch review window closes, creating undocumented MEL exposure ⚠
3.2 Review MEL and CDL items for dispatch acceptability MEL review completed ≤60 min before STD; zero dispatches with expired MEL interval items Cat B/C MEL items may impose altitude, fuel, or route restrictions that are not automatically propagated to NAVBLUE OFP, requiring manual dispatcher override and re-calculation ⚠
3.3 Verify V-speeds and performance for current configuration Performance calculations completed ≤45 min before STD; all V-speeds within aircraft flight manual limits Contaminated runway friction coefficients (SNOWTAM data) may be up to 2 hrs old; dispatcher must verify runway treatment status with station ops before confirming RTOW, as stale data can overstate runway performance by 8–12%
4.1 Receive preliminary load figures from load control Preliminary load sheet received ≥60 min before STD; ZFW variance from final ≤500 lbs Late connecting passengers and ad-hoc cargo add-ons can shift ZFW by 500–2,000 lbs after the initial fuel calculation, invalidating the filed fuel figure and requiring dispatch release amendment
4.2 Calculate final takeoff weight and CG envelope CG within ±2% MAC of certified envelope limits; TOW ≤ MTOW; load sheet issued ≤40 min before STD Bulk cargo density variances and last-minute bag pulls can cause CG exceedance not detected until ramp loading check, requiring hold reload that delays departure by 20–45 min ⚠
4.3 Perform runway-limited weight and obstacle clearance check Net takeoff flight path clears obstacles by ≥35 ft per FAR 121.189; RTOW ≥ actual TOW High-elevation airports (DEN 5,431 ft, SLC 4,227 ft) combined with high OAT frequently trigger weight restrictions that were not anticipated in the original schedule, forcing payload offload decisions at gate ⚠
5.1 Generate Operational Flight Plan and dispatch release OFP issued ≥30 min before EOBT; NOTAM safety-critical items highlighted in release NOTAM volumes at major hubs can exceed 150 active NOTAMs per airport; dispatcher must manually filter safety-critical items, creating fatigue-driven oversight risk without automated NOTAM triage tooling
5.2 Brief Captain on route, weather, NOTAMs and MEL status Crew briefing completed ≥20 min before EOBT; briefing acknowledgment logged in dispatch system At remote outstations, dispatcher-captain contact via ACARS can be disrupted by ground power failures; fallback to VHF voice briefing must be documented under FAR 121.663 joint responsibility
5.3 Captain reviews and co-signs Dispatch Release (FAR 121.663) Release co-signed ≤15 min before EOBT; 100% of departures have dual-signed release on file Under FAR 121, dispatch is a joint captain-dispatcher decision; captain fuel uplift amendments after signing trigger full release re-issue, consuming 10–20 min and risking EDCT miss ⚠
5.4 Issue final dispatch release and transmit to crew Release transmitted ≥10 min before EOBT; 100% compliance; ACARS delivery confirmation within 2 min ACARS datalink failures at remote outstations require fallback to voice release per FAR 121.663; voice releases must be manually transcribed and archived, creating audit trail gaps if not logged within 1 hr
6.1 Monitor departure, pushback and initial climb confirmation AOBT recorded within 2 min of actual event; D0 (on-time departure) target ≥78% AMS/Altéa PSS timestamp synchronization errors can misreport AOBT by 3–8 min, causing downstream crew duty time calculations to use incorrect start-of-duty reference under FAR Part 117
6.2 Track flight en-route via ACARS position reports Position report gap ≤15 min; fuel remaining vs. plan within ±500 lbs at each waypoint ACARS blackout over oceanic tracks (no VHF/SATCOM backup) leaves dispatcher without real-time fuel data for up to 60 min; oceanic fuel emergencies cannot be detected until HF radio contact is re-established ⚠
6.3 Initiate diversion or emergency response if required Diversion decision communicated to crew ≤5 min from trigger event; ground support activated ≤15 min before ETA at diversion Diversion airports at unplanned stations may lack MEL-required spare parts or certified line maintenance capability, extending AOG time by 4–24 hrs and triggering cascading schedule disruption across the network ⚠
