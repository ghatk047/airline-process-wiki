StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Receive & log corporate RFP in CRM RFP response time ≤3 business days; 100% RFPs logged in Salesforce within 24 hrs RFPs arrive via multiple untracked channels (email, broker portals, GBTA RFP Hub); manual logging causes duplicates and missed responses
1.2 Assess historical travel volume & route overlap Volume data pull completed within 4 hours of RFP receipt New corporate accounts have no booking history; reliance on self-reported volume figures that may be overstated by 20–40% to secure deeper discounts
1.3 Qualify account against minimum volume threshold Minimum qualifying threshold: 200 segments/year or $150K annual spend Sub-threshold accounts consuming senior sales capacity; no automated triage in Salesforce to enforce volume floors consistently across regions ⚠
2.1 Model discount tiers against NRM inventory Modelled revenue dilution <3% vs. unconstrained mix; analysis completed within 2 business days NRM bid-price data is point-in-time; discount tiers fixed at contract signing may become revenue-dilutive during high-demand periods without dynamic override capability
2.2 Benchmark proposed rates vs. ATPCO published fares Negotiated rate within 5–15% below lowest unrestricted published fare for target markets ATPCO tariff data has a 48-hour publication lag; competitor last-minute fare reductions not captured, causing negotiated rates to inadvertently undercut market
2.3 Validate revenue impact against load factor targets Approved rates must preserve projected route load factor ≥83% and RASK within 2% of unconstrained forecast NRM does not natively model corporate volume displacement of leisure inventory; manual override required to test scenarios on constrained peak departures ⚠
2.4 Draft contract terms and route coverage schedule Contract draft delivered to corporate buyer within 5 business days of rate approval Contract templates not version-controlled in Salesforce; legal redlines handled via email, creating misalignment between signed terms and fare filing parameters in ATPCO
3.1 File private negotiated fares in ATPCO Fare filing completed within 24 hours of contract execution; zero filing errors on first submission ATPCO private fare filing requires precise fare basis code structuring; errors in Rule 15 (Sales Restrictions) or Rule 35 (Negotiated Fares) cause incorrect TMC access or public leakage of private rates
3.2 Configure Sabre PRISM access for TMC booking PRISM configuration active and tested within 48 hours of ATPCO filing; TMC confirmation of successful test booking Sabre PRISM configuration requires manual pseudo-city mapping for each TMC location; large corporates with 50+ TMC offices create significant setup overhead with no bulk-load API
3.3 Set up NDC private offer for NDC-enabled corporates NDC offer live within 72 hours for NDC-enabled accounts; ancillary attach rate target ≥35% on NDC-booked itineraries NDC adoption among corporate TMCs remains below 30%; ATPCO and NDC offer parameters must be maintained in parallel, creating dual-maintenance risk and version drift between channels
3.4 Validate fare accessibility across all booked channels 100% fare accessibility in contracted channels before contract activation date; zero public display of private fares GDS fare display inconsistencies across Amadeus, Sabre, and Travelport due to differing cache refresh cycles (up to 4-hour lag); corporate bookers may see incorrect rates during the window ⚠
4.1 Track monthly booking volume vs. contracted commitment Volume commitment attainment reported by 5th business day of following month; data completeness ≥99% across all channels Booking data from NDC channel not consistently flowing into Redshift reconciliation pipeline; NDC-booked corporate trips may be excluded from volume commitment tracking, understating attainment
4.2 Generate quarterly corporate performance report QBR delivered to corporate buyer within 15 business days of quarter close; 100% of tier-1 accounts receive QBR Manual extraction from Redshift to Tableau for QBR preparation takes 2–3 days per account; no automated QBR generation for the long tail of mid-market accounts (typically 200+ accounts per analyst)
4.3 Assess volume commitment attainment threshold ≥85% of tier-1 accounts maintaining contracted commitment; non-compliant accounts actioned within 10 business days Contracts with volume commitment clauses rarely include enforceable penalties; non-compliant accounts may retain full discount tier without commercial consequence, eroding revenue quality ⚠
4.4 Trigger rate adjustment or incentive review Rate adjustment applied within 30 days of non-compliance trigger; targeted return to ≥85% commitment attainment within 1 quarter NRM does not have a corporate account module; rate adjustment modelling must be done manually outside the system, with no automated link between attainment tracking in Salesforce and NRM inventory controls
5.1 Initiate contract renewal 90 days before expiry 100% of active contracts have renewal opportunity opened ≥90 days before expiry; zero lapsed contracts due to missed renewal trigger Salesforce contract expiry alerts not configured for all accounts due to inconsistent contract record hygiene; lapsed contracts default to published fares, triggering reactive re-negotiation with weakened leverage
5.2 Re-model renewal rates using updated RM forecast Renewal rates modelled to deliver ≥2% RASK improvement vs. expiring contract; analysis completed 60 days before expiry 12-month forward NRM forecasts used for renewal modelling have ±8% accuracy on thin routes; locking renewal rates to inaccurate forecasts on marginal routes risks over-discounting into peak periods
5.3 Approve renewal, renegotiate, or exit account Tier-1 account renewal rate ≥90%; tier-2 renewal rate ≥75%; exit decision documented in Salesforce within 5 business days of final negotiation No systematic strategic value scoring model in Salesforce; renewal decisions on borderline accounts rely on relationship judgment rather than data, leading to inconsistent discount policy across the portfolio ⚠
