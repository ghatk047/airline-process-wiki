StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Monitor CBA amendable date and trigger opening Opening notice filed ≥180 days before CBA amendable date; 100% of contracts tracked in SuccessFactors Under the Railway Labour Act (RLA), contracts do not expire — they become 'amendable'; status quo obligation means outdated terms can persist indefinitely if airline fails to file timely, freezing modernisation of scheduling systems and work rules
1.2 Benchmark labour costs against peer carriers Data currency ≤90 days old; peer set covers ≥5 major carriers; report delivered ≥150 days before amendable date DOT Form 41 labour cost data is lagged 6–12 months; actual competitor total compensation packages (profit sharing, equity, per diem) are often not disclosed until SEC filings or union leaks, skewing benchmark modelling
1.3 Model financial impact of proposed contract changes Model covers ≥5 scenarios; labour cost projection accuracy within ±2% of actuals at year-end; model delivered 120 days before amendable date Multi-year contract costing requires fuel price and ASM growth assumptions that are highly uncertain; modelling errors >5% have triggered mid-contract re-opening demands and resulted in $100M+ unbudgeted labour cost overruns at major US carriers
2.1 Form cross-functional management negotiating team Team constituted ≥120 days before target opening session; team includes Legal, Finance, Operations, and HR representation Conflicting priorities between Operations (scheduling flexibility), Finance (cost ceiling), and HR (retention) create an incoherent management position; union negotiators routinely exploit internal inconsistencies to extract additional concessions worth 1–3% of contract value
2.2 Develop management opening proposals and costing Proposal reviewed by outside labour counsel within 10 business days; FAR Part 117 and ICAO fatigue regulation compliance verified for all scheduling provisions Airline ops constraints (FAR Part 117 rest rules, ICAO fatigue regulations, irregular operations pay) must be precisely reflected in scheduling and duty-time contract language; ambiguous drafting creates compliance liability and exposes carrier to FAA enforcement action
2.3 Obtain board approval of negotiation mandate Mandate approved before first bargaining session; cost ceiling grounded in verified FI/CO labour cost baseline Boards often set unrealistic cost ceilings based on prior-cycle assumptions or competitor announcements rather than current market data; mid-negotiation mandate revisions require additional board sessions, causing 3–6 month delays and signalling weakness to union ⚠
3.1 File Section 6 notice and open formal bargaining First bargaining session convened within 30 days of mutual Section 6 notice exchange Under the RLA status quo obligation, the airline cannot unilaterally change rates, rules, or working conditions during negotiations — a perpetual obligation that incentivises union delay tactics, with some RLA negotiations running 5–10 years before resolution
3.2 Conduct iterative proposal exchange and counter-proposals Open items log reduced by ≥10% per bargaining month; no previously agreed article reopened without written consent of both parties Aviation-specific provisions (deadhead travel standards, hotel quality tiers, irregular operation pay triggers, open-time bidding rules) generate 60–80 contested articles that resist quick resolution; pattern bargaining pressures from other carriers complicate single-carrier negotiations
3.3 Track tentative agreements and maintain open issues log TA log updated within 24 hours of each session; open issues register shared with both parties within 48 hours Without a structured tracker, previously agreed articles get reopened through 'linked bargaining' tactics, extending negotiations by 3–6 months; several major US airline negotiations have collapsed at the TA stage due to tracking failures
3.4 Assess bargaining progress and declare impasse if warranted ≥75% of articles TA'd before NMB referral; target contract duration ≤24 months from opening notice to ratification The 2022–2024 US pilot shortage dramatically increased union leverage, extending pilot contract negotiations by 18+ months beyond historical norms; airlines that delayed impasse declarations lost scheduling certainty during peak summer hiring and capacity planning cycles ⚠
4.1 Request NMB mediation and establish case docket NMB application filed within 5 business days of impasse declaration; first mediated session convened within 45 days of NMB acceptance NMB mediation under the RLA is open-ended with no statutory time limit; airlines have been in NMB mediation for 3–7 years, making 5-year fleet planning, pilot hiring targets, and network capacity decisions highly uncertain throughout
4.2 Conduct NMB-mediated bargaining and resolve open articles Open articles reduced by ≥50% within 90 days of mediation start; full TA reached before proffer of arbitration where possible NMB proffer of arbitration triggers a mandatory 30-day cooling-off period after which 'self-help' (strikes, lockouts, unilateral change) becomes legal — the resulting operational and reputational risk has cost airlines $500M–$2B in market cap losses within 48 hours of strike announcements ⚠
5.1 Draft and execute full tentative agreement Legal review completed within 15 business days; zero unresolved ambiguous provisions; implementation timeline agreed before union ratification vote Ambiguous contract language in scheduling system provisions (e.g. how crew management software generates open-time offers, or how irregular ops pay is calculated from ACARS data) becomes the primary source of grievances within 90 days of implementation, driving arbitration costs
5.2 Conduct union membership ratification vote Voter turnout ≥60%; ratification threshold >50% of votes cast per union bylaws; result certified within 5 business days of vote close Failed ratification votes (e.g. American Airlines pilot TA rejected in 2023 requiring renegotiation) delay implementation by 6–18 months, trigger stock price decline of 3–8%, and force management back to bargaining with a weakened position and increased union expectations ⚠
6.1 Implement ratified agreement in HR and payroll systems All pay changes live within 30 days of ratification; retroactive pay (if applicable) paid within 60 days; payroll error rate <0.1% of affected employees Complex retroactive pay calculations (back pay owed from amendable date, potentially 1–4 years prior, to thousands of employees) require manual reconciliation when SAP SuccessFactors lacks a native retro-pay engine for custom union pay scales, creating $10M+ reconciliation risk
6.2 Distribute contract and conduct manager training Contract accessible to 100% of covered employees within 14 days of ratification; ≥90% of frontline managers trained within 30 days; training completion tracked in SuccessFactors LMS Inconsistent manager interpretation of new scheduling provisions and irregular ops pay rules is the primary source of early grievances post-ratification; structured manager briefing has been shown to reduce first-year grievance rate by ~30% at comparable carriers
6.3 Monitor grievance filings and manage compliance Step 1 grievance response within 10 business days; ≥70% of grievances resolved at Step 1 or 2 without arbitration; arbitration spend <$500K per year per contract group Accumulation of unresolved grievances — particularly around scheduling systems and irregular ops — creates a poisoned industrial relations climate entering the next contract cycle, amplifying union leverage; airlines with >500 open grievances at amendable date face 15–25% higher settlement premiums ⚠
