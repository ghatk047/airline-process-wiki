StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Extract fuel volume forecast from flight schedule Forecast accuracy ≥95% vs actual uplift volume at month-end Schedule changes post-freeze invalidate fuel pre-purchases with no automated re-forecast trigger in SkyWORKS; manual rework required for each schedule revision
1.2 Model price scenarios and set hedging target Hedge ratio within board-approved policy band of 30–70% of forward 12-month volume Jet fuel crack spread volatility (2022: +180% YoY) can force hedge ratio outside approved bands requiring board escalation; no automated alert in OpenLink Endur ⚠
1.3 Submit annual fuel budget to Finance Budget variance ≤±5% vs actual fuel cost at year-end Annual budget locked in Q4 but jet fuel prices move daily; SAP CO-PA budget version does not auto-update with market rate changes, causing stale variance reporting throughout the year
2.1 Evaluate and qualify fuel suppliers by station ≥2 qualified suppliers at every Tier-1 hub; 100% supplier compliance with ASTM D1655 specification Remote and international stations frequently have a single into-plane agent with no qualified alternative; supply disruption at these stations forces flight hold with no automated contingency in SAP Ariba ⚠
2.2 Issue RFQ and evaluate competitive fuel bids RFQ cycle time ≤21 calendar days; minimum 3 bids received per major hub station Fuel suppliers submit non-standard pricing structures (flat-rate, index-linked, spot differential) making direct price comparison difficult; no normalisation tool in SAP Ariba Sourcing for jet fuel indices
2.3 Negotiate and execute fuel supply agreement Contract coverage ≥90% of annual volume; contract execution ≤30 days from award decision Into-plane agent contracts are separate from fuel supply contracts at most airports — coordinating two parallel contract cycles per station doubles procurement workload and creates liability gap periods ⚠
3.1 Generate daily fuel uplift orders from flight ops Order accuracy ≥99.5% (uplift quantity within ±2% of flight plan requirement); order generation completed ≥4 hours before first departure Tankering decisions require real-time station price feed integration with FuelPlus; feed latency of 2–6 hours causes suboptimal tankering recommendations and missed cost-saving windows
3.2 Evaluate tankering opportunity vs payload penalty Tankering savings ≥$0.01/US gallon average across network; zero overweight departure events attributable to tankering Boeing 737 MAX 8 MTOW constraints limit tankering payload trade-off on high-load routes; FuelPlus does not automatically model per-aircraft-variant weight limits, requiring manual override by dispatcher ⚠
3.3 Execute purchase order and payment authorisation PO-to-supplier-confirmation cycle ≤4 hours for same-day uplifts; zero declined fuel card transactions International stations use Air BP or Shell Aviation fuel cards with daily spending limits that are frequently exceeded during irregular operations; no real-time limit monitoring integrated with SAP Ariba
4.1 Monitor real-time fuel delivery at departure gate Fuelling completion ≥30 minutes before scheduled departure; zero misfuel incidents ACARS delivery confirmations are occasionally lost or delayed at high-traffic hub stations; manual phone backup creates audit trail gaps that complicate invoice reconciliation and incident investigation
4.2 Validate fuel quality certificate at delivery 100% batch documentation compliance before uplift; zero fuel contamination incidents per year API gravity and water content test results take 2–4 hours at some stations — aircraft may be fuelled before final QC sign-off is recorded, creating regulatory exposure under EASA Part-M and FAA AC 43-12A ⚠
4.3 Manage fuel shortage or delivery failure exception Shortage resolution ≤45 minutes; zero flight cancellations attributable to fuel unavailability At single-supplier airports there is no fallback into-plane agent; shortage forces a weight-limited departure or flight hold with no automated contingency order workflow in FuelPlus — manual station calls required ⚠
5.1 3-way match fuel invoice to uplift and PO Automated 3-way match rate ≥97%; discrepancy identification within 2 business days of invoice receipt Into-plane agents invoice using different unit conventions (US gallons, litres, imperial gallons) with varying density references; unit conversion errors are the primary cause of auto-match failures and require manual correction ⚠
5.2 Resolve invoice discrepancy with fuel supplier Discrepancy recovery rate ≥95% within 30 days; average credit recovery ≥$0.005/gallon on disputed volume International suppliers dispute density conversion differences citing local regulatory standards — no standard IATA API gravity reference is integrated into the SAP Ariba supplier collaboration workflow, prolonging resolution cycles
5.3 Process approved fuel payment and post to GL On-time payment rate ≥98% per contract terms; zero duplicate payment incidents per quarter Multi-currency fuel payments (USD, EUR, GBP, AED) require daily FX rate updates in SAP — rate feed lag of 1–2 days creates accounting timing differences vs actual bank settlement, generating reconciling items in period-end close
6.1 Mark-to-market hedge positions and test effectiveness Hedge effectiveness ≥80% (IFRS 9 hedge accounting qualification threshold); MTM report delivered by 09:00 each trading day IFRS 9 hedge accounting requires both prospective and retrospective effectiveness testing — OpenLink Endur configuration requires quarterly recertification by external auditor; any system upgrade risks de-qualifying existing hedges ⚠
6.2 Report fuel cost per ASM to senior leadership Fuel CASM ≤$0.055; variance report published within 5 business days of month-end close Flight-level fuel cost attribution requires a data join between FuelPlus uplift records and SkyWORKS ASM data with no automated ETL pipeline — monthly manual extract and reconciliation takes 2–3 FTE days and introduces version control risk
