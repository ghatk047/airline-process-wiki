Slide 3 of 3 — KPIs & Risk Register
StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Subscribe to FAA rulemaking & ICAO SARPs updates 100% of applicable NPRMs captured within 5 business days of publication FAA issues 300+ NPRMs per year; manual triage against 14 CFR Part 119/121 scope causes missed applicability deadlines without automated rule-matching
1.2 Classify regulatory change by AOC impact category Classification completed within 3 business days of receipt; ≥95% accuracy validated by VP Safety review 14 CFR Part 121 overlaps with Part 91, Part 135, and ICAO Annex 6 — misclassification risk is high for carriers operating international routes under bilateral agreements ⚠
1.3 Determine material impact on AOC or OpSpecs conditions Decision documented within 2 business days; 0 unreviewed critical items older than 5 days OpSpecs are issued at aircraft-type level — a single Boeing 737 MAX amendment may trigger parallel OpSpec revisions across A002, B036, and D085 series, each requiring separate FAA FSDO concurrence ⚠
2.1 Conduct OpSpecs gap analysis against new regulation Gap analysis completed ≥30 days before regulation effective date for non-emergency rules WebOPSS does not provide automated diff between existing OpSpecs and new regulatory text — analysts must perform manual line-by-line comparison across 100+ OpSpec paragraphs, consuming 40+ analyst-hours per major rule change
2.2 Review fleet type ratings and pilot training compliance 100% of pilots current on type rating within 24-month recurrent cycle; 0 flights dispatched with lapsed qualification Introduction of new flight deck technology (e.g., Boeing 737 MAX MCAS differences training) requires FSDO-approved training programme amendments before revenue flying — timeline mismatch with aircraft delivery schedules creates capacity risk
2.3 Identify non-compliance gaps and remediation requirements Zero Critical-rated gaps open beyond 30 days; High-rated gaps resolved within 90 days FAA Airworthiness Directives issued via AD registry may conflict with manufacturer service bulletin schedules — carriers must negotiate alternate means of compliance (AMOC) approval which averages 6–18 months with FAA Aircraft Certification Office ⚠
2.4 Develop and approve corrective action plan (CAP) CAP approved by VP Safety within 10 business days of gap identification; milestone adherence ≥90% CAP funding competes with capital allocation cycles — safety-driven OpEx spend cannot be deferred but lacks the revenue justification framework used for commercial projects, creating budget approval delays ⚠
3.1 Prepare AOC amendment or OpSpecs revision package Package completeness score ≥95% on first submission (measured by FAA deficiency rate); zero incomplete submissions FAA Form 8900-1 and WebOPSS data entry does not validate cross-references between OpSpec series — errors discovered during FSDO review require full resubmission and add 4–8 weeks to approval timeline
3.2 Submit amendment package to FAA FSDO via WebOPSS Submission made ≥60 days before effective date for Major amendments; ≥30 days for Minor WebOPSS portal has documented uptime issues during peak submission periods (e.g., end-of-year deadline rush); carriers with sole-source FSDO assignment have no alternative submission channel if POI is on extended leave
3.3 Respond to FAA requests for additional information RAI response submitted within 10 business days of receipt; first-response resolution rate ≥75% FAA POI reviewer rotation means institutional knowledge of airline-specific OpSpecs is lost — new POI may re-open previously resolved items, causing amendment cycles to restart and exceeding planned compliance timelines ⚠
3.4 Receive, validate, and file approved OpSpecs revision Distribution to operational departments within 24 hours of FAA approval; acknowledgement receipt rate 100% within 48 hours OpSpecs revisions must be cascaded to Dispatch, Crew Training, Maintenance, and Ground Ops simultaneously — document management systems (SharePoint) lack mandatory-read workflow enforcement, creating compliance traceability gaps
4.1 Conduct periodic SMS internal compliance audit 4 SMS audits completed per year; critical findings closed within 30 days; audit coverage ≥100% of SMS components annually FAA requires SMS programmes to demonstrate safety performance improvement over time (AC 120-92B) — carriers using spreadsheet-based audit tracking cannot produce trend evidence required for IOSA (IATA Operational Safety Audit) recertification
4.2 Analyse FOQA safety performance indicators against thresholds FOQA data capture rate ≥99.5% of revenue flights; SPI exceedance rate <0.5 per 1,000 flights for high-severity events FOQA data volumes from 800+ daily flights generate 2TB+ per month — without automated anomaly detection (e.g., Palantir Aviation Safety), manual analyst review creates a 4–6 week lag between exceedance event and corrective action initiation ⚠
4.3 File ASAP or VDRP voluntary safety disclosure report ASAP report submission within 24 hours of event identification; ERC review completed within 30 days; 0 reports rejected for lateness ASAP programme confidentiality protections (14 CFR §13.401) conflict with labour contract grievance procedures — ambiguity over which events qualify for ASAP immunity vs. disciplinary action suppresses voluntary disclosure rates, undermining SMS effectiveness ⚠
5.1 Update MEL, Operations Manual, and training materials MEL and Ops Manual updates published within 5 business days of OpSpecs approval; EFB content sync to 100% of fleet devices within 24 hours MEL amendment cycle requires simultaneous approval from airline, Boeing (MMEL source), and FAA — three-party review adds 60–120 days per amendment, creating temporary deferred maintenance item backlogs that inflate maintenance cost and aircraft availability risk
5.2 Track and report compliance costs against regulatory budget Regulatory compliance budget variance ≤5% quarterly; cost-per-amendment tracked and benchmarked annually Regulatory compliance costs are scattered across Safety, Legal, Crew Training, and Maintenance cost centres in SAP — absence of a dedicated regulatory compliance WBS element prevents accurate total-cost-of-compliance reporting required by board audit committees
5.3 Archive compliance records and close regulatory cycle 100% of compliance records archived within 5 business days of cycle close; retrieval time for auditor requests ≤1 business day 14 CFR §121.380 mandates 7-year retention for most compliance records, but GDPR and state privacy laws impose conflicting deletion requirements for records containing crew personal data — dual-obligation conflict requires manual legal review for each retention decision
