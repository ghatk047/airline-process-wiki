StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Extract flight schedule & compute GSE demand GSE demand forecast accuracy ≥95% vs actual utilisation AMS schedule feeds lag 2–4 hr behind real-time slot changes; GSE demand mismatches occur at congested airports during irregular operations
1.2 Allocate GSE units to flights via AMS rules GSE assignment completion ≥60 min before first departure of each wave; compliance ≥95% Manual supervisor overrides bypass AMS allocation logic, creating last-minute equipment conflicts during irregular operations and peak waves
1.3 Confirm GSE serviceability against maintenance roster GSE fleet serviceability rate ≥92% at start of each operational day AMOS maintenance records not always updated in real time by field technicians; apparent serviceability rates overstate actual availability, discovered only at stand ⚠
2.1 Pre-position GSE at assigned stand before block-in On-stand pre-positioning compliance ≥95% of turns; late positioning incidents ≤2% per month GPS-based GSE telematics not universally deployed; manual radio check-in introduces positioning errors during peak waves, causing stand conflicts
2.2 Conduct pre-use walk-around inspection per IATA AHM 913 Pre-use inspection completion rate 100%; open defects from prior shift actioned before use Inspection checklist compliance drops during early-morning wave peaks; IATA AHM 913 requires 100% completion but enforcement is manual and shift-dependent ⚠
2.3 Validate GPU/ACU electrical output and hose integrity GPU/ACU pre-use pass rate ≥98%; voltage within ±2% of 115V AC / 400 Hz spec Aging GPU units exhibit voltage fluctuations not detected by manual test protocols; risk of avionics damage if aircraft switched from APU to non-compliant ground power
3.1 Connect GPU and switch aircraft to ground power on block-in Aircraft on ground power within 3 min of block-in; APU shutdown compliance ≥90% of turns Ground power connection delays extend APU run time; each additional minute of APU operation costs ~$12–15 in fuel and increases CO₂ emissions beyond CORSIA allowances
3.2 Deploy belt loaders and baggage tractors for cargo transfer Belt loader/tractor on-stand on-time rate ≥95%; FOD incidents attributable to GSE ≤0 per quarter Belt loader height mis-calibration causes FOD risk and potential cargo damage; no automated alignment verification in SITA AMS — relies on ramp agent visual check
3.3 Position fueling equipment and coordinate refueling start Fueling equipment on-stand within 10 min of block-in; fueling completion ≥15 min before STD Fueling equipment scheduling not integrated with AMS turnaround timer; manual coordination via radio introduces sequencing delays that cascade to departure time
4.1 Log GSE defect in maintenance system with fault code Defect logged within 15 min of identification; compliance ≥98%; zero unlogged defects on end-of-day fleet check Field staff reluctant to log minor defects fearing shift-end paperwork burden; systematic under-reporting creates hidden fleet degradation trends undetected until failure
4.2 Assess safety criticality and ground unit if required Safety-critical GSE grounded within 30 min of defect identification; zero safety-critical units operated beyond grounding threshold No standardised IATA or ICAO safety-criticality matrix for GSE defects; airlines use proprietary criteria, creating inconsistency during auditor spot-checks and insurance reviews ⚠
4.3 Execute corrective maintenance and post-repair check Corrective maintenance turnaround time ≤4 hr for critical GSE; ≤24 hr for non-critical; first-time fix rate ≥85% Spare parts availability for aging diesel GSE (10+ yr pushback tugs, belt loaders) declining as OEMs shift to electric-only product lines; repair lead times increasing 30–60 days ⚠
4.4 Update GSE fleet availability status in AMS Fleet availability status updated within 1 hr of repair closure; AMS–AMOS sync accuracy ≥99% SITA AMS availability flags not automatically synced from AMOS work order closure; manual dual-entry required, introducing status lag and risk of re-assigning grounded units
5.1 Compile monthly GSE utilisation and MTBF KPIs GSE MTBF ≥500 operating hr; fleet downtime rate ≤3%; dashboard published by 5th of each month GSE telematics data not consistently ingested into Tableau; units lacking IoT sensors require manual log extraction, creating fleet visibility gaps in the monthly KPI pack
5.2 Assess fleet adequacy and identify replacement candidates Fleet adequacy ratio ≥1.2× peak demand; assets beyond 10-yr economic life flagged for replacement within next budget cycle Capital expenditure approval for GSE replacement requires 12–18 month lead time via SAP Ariba; aging fleet regularly operates beyond economic life, increasing failure risk and MRO cost ⚠
5.3 Initiate GSE procurement or lease request via SAP Ariba Procurement lead time ≤90 days for standard GSE; ≤180 days for electric/specialist units Airport authority GSE concession agreements at certain airports restrict airline-owned equipment, requiring third-party GSE sourcing at higher cost and lower operational control
