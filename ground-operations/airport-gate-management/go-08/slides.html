StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Receive IRROP alert from Operations Control Centre Alert-to-acknowledgement time ≤5 min SITA AMS alert feeds from multiple upstream systems (NOC, Jeppesen, Altéa) with inconsistent data latency, causing duplicate or conflicting IRROP triggers during high-volume disruption events
1.2 Classify disruption type and assign severity tier Classification accuracy ≥95% (validated vs. final outcome) Mechanical IRROPs often escalate from Tier 1 to Tier 3 mid-process due to late MX diagnosis; premature low-tier classification under-triggers rebooking resources ⚠
1.3 Confirm affected flight status in Amadeus Altéa PSS Manifest retrieval time ≤2 min from alert Altéa PSS record locking conflicts when OCC and airport teams access the same flight simultaneously; requires coordinated queue management via PSS administrator role
2.1 Extract full passenger manifest for affected flight Manifest extraction completeness 100% of checked-in and booked passengers Passengers booked via third-party OTAs often have incomplete contact data in Altéa, blocking automated notification and requiring manual agent outreach
2.2 Flag PRM, UM, and MAAS passengers requiring priority handling 100% of SSR-flagged passengers identified before rebooking batch runs SSR codes entered by codeshare or interline partners are not always carried over into the operating carrier's Altéa record, creating missed-flag risk for vulnerable passengers ⚠
2.3 Determine EU261 / DOT compensation threshold applicability Eligibility determination completed before rebooking batch execution EU261 extraordinary-circumstances defence (e.g. ATC strike, severe weather) requires real-time legal review; automated rules engines in Altéa do not always capture all exemption nuances, exposing the airline to over- or under-payment ⚠
3.1 Execute automated mass rebooking via Altéa Inventory Auto-rebook success rate ≥70% of affected PAX; batch processing time ≤15 min Inventory availability for the rebooking window is often constrained by already-high load factors (industry avg ≥83%), forcing passengers onto flights departing 24–48 hrs later and increasing duty-of-care cost ⚠
3.2 Identify and triage rebooking conflicts and seat oversells Oversell resolution time ≤30 min; upgrade/interline acceptance rate ≥60% NRM inventory controls may auto-close cabin classes during the rebooking wave, preventing agents from placing passengers even when physical seats are available — requires temporary override authorisation from RM Analyst ⚠
3.3 Manually rebook complex and unresolvable itineraries Manual rebook completion ≤60 min from conflict identification Interline rebooking requires bilateral interline agreements (IIAs); if the next-available flight is on a carrier with no IIA, agents must issue refunds rather than rebooking, increasing passenger dissatisfaction scores
3.4 Issue new boarding passes and update ancillary entitlements Boarding pass issuance ≤5 min per passenger post-rebooking confirmation Ancillary transfer from disrupted to alternate flight is not fully automated in Altéa DCS; agents manually re-enter seat selections and meal preferences, adding ~3–4 min per PNR and creating ancillary revenue leakage
4.1 Trigger automated SMS and email notifications to affected PAX Notification delivery rate ≥95% within 10 min of rebooking confirmation Passengers with international mobile numbers often fail SMS delivery due to carrier routing gaps; email-only fallback is slower and has lower open rates during time-sensitive IRROP events ⚠
4.2 Update FIDS and gate display boards with disruption status FIDS update within 3 min of disruption classification confirmation SITA AMS FIDS integration with some airport authority display networks requires manual refresh at certain stations, creating information lag of 5–15 min in secondary terminals
4.3 Brief gate agents and customer service desk on disruption status Agent briefing completion ≤10 min from IRROP classification High-disruption events (weather cancellations affecting 20+ flights simultaneously) overwhelm customer service desk staffing ratios — airline contingency plans often require minimum 1:50 agent-to-passenger ratio, which is rarely met in mass IRROP scenarios
5.1 Assess duty-of-care entitlements by delay duration and cause Entitlement assessment completed within 15 min of Tier 2/3 classification EU261 duty-of-care obligations apply regardless of extraordinary-circumstances defence — airlines frequently conflate compensation exemption with duty-of-care exemption, creating regulatory exposure under EC Regulation 261/2004 Art. 9 ⚠
5.2 Issue meal and hotel vouchers to entitled passengers Voucher issuance rate ≥90% of entitled passengers within 30 min of threshold trigger Many airlines operate VMS as a standalone system disconnected from Altéa PNR records; reconciling voucher costs against individual PNRs for EU261 cost recovery requires manual matching, creating audit gaps of up to 30% unreconciled spend
5.3 Manage passenger queue flow at rebooking and check-in desks Average passenger wait time at rebooking desk ≤20 min during IRROP event SITA AMS resource scheduling module does not dynamically redeploy staff from non-disrupted gates during an IRROP event — staffing reallocation depends on supervisor radio coordination, adding 15–20 min to response time
6.1 Record disruption costs against flight and cost centre in SAP IRROP cost posting completed within 24 hrs of event closure Voucher costs from third-party hotel partners arrive as bulk invoices weekly, preventing real-time flight-level cost attribution in SAP CO and distorting disruption cost-per-passenger metrics
6.2 Submit EU261 / DOT compensation claims data to legal team Claims data submitted within 48 hrs of event; EU261 response SLA ≤7 days per regulation ATPCO and IATA resolution frameworks for extraordinary-circumstances documentation are not standardised; each jurisdiction's CAA applies different evidence standards, requiring airline legal teams to maintain jurisdiction-specific defence packs
6.3 Generate IRROP performance report and update process KPIs Report published within 72 hrs; cost-per-disrupted-passenger target ≤$85 (industry benchmark) Data from SITA AMS, Altéa, VMS, and SAP sit in separate schemas with no native ETL integration; Tableau dashboards rely on nightly batch feeds, preventing real-time IRROP performance monitoring during the event itself
