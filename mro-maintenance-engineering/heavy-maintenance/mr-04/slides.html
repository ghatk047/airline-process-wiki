StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Generate parts demand forecast from maintenance programme Forecast accuracy ≥85% (MAPE ≤15%) Fleet mix changes (737 MAX vs 737-800 intermix) cause demand volatility; AMOS forecast module requires manual adjustment for new-type introductions where consumption history is sparse
1.2 Validate forecast against consumption history and ADs Forecast review cycle completed within 5 business days Sporadic demand items (rotables with <4 removals/year) produce unreliable AMOS statistical forecasts; manual override requires SME judgement and is not auditable within AMOS ⚠
1.3 Submit demand plan to procurement and finance for budget approval Budget approval lead time ≤10 business days No native AMOS–SAP interface at many carriers; demand plan must be manually re-keyed into SAP MRP, introducing transcription errors and plan drift between systems
2.1 Check stock availability against net demand requirements Stock-out rate ≤2% for critical AOG-flagged parts Inventory accuracy degrades when technicians issue parts without closing work orders in AMOS; phantom stock causes missed AOG events and emergency buys that erode savings from planned procurement ⚠
2.2 Source parts from approved vendor list via RFQ Vendor lead time compliance ≥90%; RFQ issued within 1 business day of requirement FAA/EASA Part 145 approved supplier list is slow to update; unapproved parts risk is highest on spot-market buys where non-qualified vendors are used to satisfy urgent demand
2.3 Evaluate supplier quotes and select preferred source PO cycle time ≤3 business days (AOG: ≤4 hours) AOG situations force bypass of normal sourcing controls; expedited buys from non-preferred vendors increase counterfeit-part risk and undermine FAR Part 145 traceability requirements ⚠
2.4 Issue purchase order and confirm supplier delivery commitment PO-to-delivery on-time rate ≥88% International freight delays for OEM parts (Boeing, CFM International); post-2022 supply chain disruption continues to affect lead times for 737 MAX-specific components, inflating AOG exposure
3.1 Receive parts shipment and verify airworthiness documentation Receiving processing time ≤4 hours for AOG-flagged parts; ≤24 hours for routine receipts Incomplete or missing FAA 8130-3 / EASA Form 1 is common on spot-market purchases; quarantine and supplier chase delays average 3–5 days, directly extending AOG events
3.2 Inspect parts for airworthiness and serviceable condition Inspection turn-time ≤8 hours; incoming rejection rate ≤3% Counterfeit parts detection requires detailed visual and dimensional checks; AMOS quality module lacks integration with industry counterfeit databases (IATA TACT, EASA SACA) forcing manual cross-reference ⚠
3.3 Quarantine non-conforming parts and raise supplier NCR NCR resolution cycle ≤15 business days; quarantined parts segregated within 1 hour of rejection Quarantine storage space is constrained at line stations; disposal of life-limited parts requires documented destruction to prevent re-entry into supply chain per FAA Order 8120.11
4.1 Assign bin location and update AMOS inventory record Inventory location accuracy ≥99.5%; bin assignment completed within 2 hours of acceptance Barcode label degradation in hangar environments (heat, hydraulic fluid) causes scan failures and phantom inventory; manual overrides bypass AMOS audit trail, creating traceability gaps
4.2 Monitor shelf-life, expiry dates, and life-limited part counters Shelf-life compliance 100%; zero expired parts issued from serviceable stock Elastomeric seals and composite consumables have 12–24 month shelf lives; AMOS expiry alerts require manual acknowledgement — risk of expired parts being issued if alert is dismissed without action ⚠
4.3 Conduct periodic cycle counts and reconcile inventory discrepancies Inventory accuracy post-count ≥99%; cycle count variance ≤1% by value High-velocity consumables (O-rings, safety wire, lockwire) are difficult to count accurately; inventory write-offs require Finance (SAP FI) approval causing multi-day delays before AMOS quantities are corrected
5.1 Receive and validate parts request from maintenance work order Parts picking initiated within 30 minutes of WO request submission Late parts requests from technicians (defects discovered mid-task) disrupt shop flow; AMOS WO-to-stores interface requires sub-task closure before parts issuance, slowing technician access during time-critical events
5.2 Kit and stage parts at work centre before task start Kitting accuracy ≥99%; kit staged ≥1 hour before scheduled task start Last-minute P/N substitutions (alternate part approved for use) require a Quality Inspector re-verification before issuance; kitting errors discovered at point-of-use cause unplanned AOG delays averaging 2–4 hours ⚠
5.3 Issue parts to technician and record WO consumption in AMOS Parts issuance documentation completeness 100%; removed parts tagged within 1 hour of removal Technicians informally borrowing parts without AMOS issuance record causes inventory inaccuracy and traceability failures — a regulatory non-compliance risk under FAR Part 43 and EASA Part 145.A.42
6.1 Tag and track removed unserviceable rotable in AMOS Rotable U/S tagging compliance 100%; AMOS update completed within 4 hours of removal Missing part histories on purchased used rotables from secondary market; AMOS rotable module requires manual re-entry of pre-purchase records from prior operators, with no standardised data exchange format
6.2 Assess repair viability against BER threshold BER rate ≤8% of annual rotable pool; average repair cost vs. new-unit cost ≤60% OEM shop visit pricing (especially CFM International LEAP engine components) is non-transparent and has risen >25% since 2022 due to supply shortages; BER thresholds require quarterly recalibration to remain accurate ⚠
6.3 Ship core to approved repair agency and track shop visit TAT Repair TAT ≤30 days for avionics LRUs; ≤60 days for engine accessory components Third-party MRO shop visit visibility is limited; AMOS shop visit module does not receive real-time status feeds from external MRO providers — manual status chasing required, with average 10-day response lag inflating fleet-level rotable pool size
6.4 Receive repaired rotable and return to serviceable stock Rotable pool availability rate ≥92%; receiving and return-to-stock processing ≤4 hours Repaired units occasionally returned at a different modification standard or with higher-than-quoted cost; configuration mismatch requires re-inspection and potential work-order revision, delaying return to pool by 1–3 days ⚠
