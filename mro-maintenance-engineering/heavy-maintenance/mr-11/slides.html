1.1 Initiate structural damage finding report DFR opened within 2 hours of discovery; 100% of findings logged before repair initiation Manual damage measurement and paper sketches introduce transcription errors when uploaded to AMOS; no integrated photo capture workflow forces separate file attachment steps
1.2 Define NDT method and inspection scope NDT scope defined within 4 hours of DFR; SRM chapter referenced on 100% of instructions Boeing Toolbox revision currency lag — SRM updates from Boeing Service Bulletins (SBs) can trail physical publication by 30–60 days, risking use of superseded acceptance limits
1.3 Evaluate damage against SRM allowable limits Disposition decision issued within 8 hours of DFR for non-AOG; 100% of beyond-limit findings escalated same shift Ambiguous SRM allowable tables for compound damage (multiple adjacent findings) require engineering judgement; misclassification risk if junior structures engineers make disposition calls without Level III review ⚠
2.1 Submit damage for engineering disposition review EDR submitted within 2 hours of beyond-limits determination; completeness score ≥95% (all required fields populated) AMOS EDR workflow does not integrate directly with OEM damage disposition portals (Boeing SRM Online); engineers must duplicate data entry across two systems, increasing cycle time and error risk
2.2 Evaluate repair options against approved engineering data Engineering disposition issued within 24 hours for non-AOG; 100% of dispositions reference specific SRM paragraph or approved data source Recurring structural damage at frame stations may indicate systemic fatigue or corrosion issues not captured in individual DFRs; lack of fleet-level damage trending in Boeing Toolbox means repeat repairs go undetected without manual cross-referencing
2.3 Determine if OEM or DER engineering order is required OEM referral decision made within 4 hours of disposition review; 0 instances of in-house repair where OEM EO was required by AD or AWL Airlines with limited ODA (Organization Designation Authorization) scope must route repairs to Boeing, incurring 5–15 business day lead times that drive AOG extensions; DER authority matrix not always current in AMOS, causing avoidable OEM referrals ⚠
2.4 Issue Structural Repair Order and material requirements SRO issued within 2 hours of disposition approval; material list completeness ≥98% (no unplanned AOG parts requests during repair) AMOS material reservation does not lock AOG-priority alloy sheet stock, allowing concurrent heavy-check work orders to consume materials needed for structural repairs, causing unplanned procurement delays
3.1 Assign certified NDT Level II/III inspector to task Inspector assigned within 1 hour of work instruction release; 100% of assignments verified against NAS410 certification currency NDT Level II/III certifications require 24-month renewal (NAS410); gaps in recency tracking within AMOS qualification module mean expired or method-restricted inspectors may be assigned without automated block, creating FAA Part 145 compliance exposure
3.2 Execute NDT inspection per approved work instruction Coverage rate ≥100% of defined zone (no uninspected area); calibration performed every 4 hours or per shift change; scan data archived within 30 minutes of inspection completion Phased-array UT (PAUT) equipment data files (Olympus OmniScan .evo format) are not natively ingestible by AMOS, requiring manual transcription of indication sizes and locations — primary source of sizing errors reported in ASIAS NDT occurrence database
3.3 Assess NDT findings against work instruction acceptance criteria Level III review completed within 4 hours of inspection; 0 indications accepted beyond SRM limits without engineering sign-off Secondary damage found during NDT (e.g., cracking beyond a corrosion blister) resets the engineering disposition cycle, adding 24–72 hours to check schedule with no pre-planned buffer in most C-check work packages ⚠
3.4 Archive NDT findings and close NDT work card Work card closed within 2 hours of Level III acceptance; 100% of scan data files attached to aircraft serial number record AMOS document storage size limits (per-record attachment caps) require off-system archiving of large PAUT data files to network shares, breaking single-source traceability and complicating FAA audit responses
4.1 Procure certified repair materials and special fasteners Material kitting completed within 4 hours of SRO release for planned repairs; 100% of issued materials have traceable Certificate of Conformance High-strength aluminium alloy sheet (2024-T3, 7075-T6) and titanium fasteners have long procurement lead times (8–16 weeks); inadequate buffer stock planning in SAP MRP causes AOG parts requests that extend check TAT by 3–5 days
4.2 Execute structural repair per approved Structural Repair Order Repair step completion sign-off within SRO-defined sequence; 0 out-of-sequence step completions; fastener installation torque within ±10% of SRM specification Complex composite-to-metal bonded repairs require temperature-controlled adhesive curing (120°C bond-line) with limited cure oven capacity during peak C-check input; scheduling conflicts with concurrent composites repairs delay structural repair cycle by 12–24 hours
4.3 Verify repair dimensions and load path restoration 100% of SRO-defined hold points signed before next step begins; dimensional non-conformance rate <2% of structural repair work orders AMOS hold-point enforcement relies on inspector discipline — system allows step sign-off without prior hold-point completion if sequences are not correctly locked, enabling inadvertent bypass of critical structural inspection gates ⚠
4.4 Apply corrosion inhibitor and sealant compound Sealant applied within 30 minutes of surface preparation (ASTM D1002 open-time limit); cure time logged per batch number; 100% coverage of faying surfaces per SRM specification Sealant pot-life management under hangar temperature variations (summer: 35°C+) accelerates cure and reduces workable window; inadequate ambient monitoring in maintenance hangars leads to sealant application beyond open-time limits, requiring removal and rework
5.1 Perform post-repair NDT verification scan Post-repair scan coverage ≥100% of repair footprint + 25mm margin; indication-free result confirmed on 100% of primary structural repairs before inspector sign-off Sealant application over fastener rows reduces PAUT coupling effectiveness, causing near-surface dead zones that may mask shallow cracking at fastener holes; no airline-standardised post-sealant scan procedure in SRM forces ad-hoc method selection by NDT Level III
5.2 Quality inspector independent review and dual sign-off Independent QA review completed within 4 hours of last work card sign-off; QA deficiency rate <1% of released structural repair work orders Dual-sign requirements under FAR Part 145 and EASA Part-145 mandate independent inspection, but AMOS does not prevent the original mechanic from also serving as the independent inspector if role segregation is not enforced at the user account level — a recurring finding in FAA Part 145 audits
5.3 Verify documentation completeness and regulatory compliance Documentation review completed within 2 hours of QA release; 0 aircraft released to service with open documentation deficiencies Structural repairs subject to Airworthiness Limitation (AWL) items require recurring inspection intervals to be loaded into AMOS task scheduling at time of repair closure — failure to create the repeat task results in missed mandatory AWL inspections, a critical safety and Part 121 compliance failure ⚠
5.4 Update airworthiness records and issue RTS authorisation RTS issued within 1 hour of documentation approval; 100% of DER repairs accompanied by filed FAA Form 337 within 8 hours of RTS; AWL recurring tasks created and confirmed in AMOS for 100% of limit-item repairs FAA Form 337 electronic filing via FAA ACAIS portal is not integrated with AMOS, requiring manual data re-entry and introducing 2–4 hour delays at peak check closure periods; lost or misfiled 337s create airworthiness record gaps that require costly FAA coordination to resolve
