1.1 Compile CAMO annual audit programme 100% of Part-145 approved providers audited within 24-month cycle; schedule published ≥60 days before Q1 MRO provider network spans 15+ stations; manual calendar alignment in spreadsheets leads to audit gaps when contracts are added mid-year without AMOS notification trigger
1.2 Risk-score providers using performance data Risk model refresh completed ≤5 business days after each completed audit; High-risk providers flagged within 48 hours Reliability data is siloed across AMOS and airline OTP systems; manual data extraction introduces 1–2 week lag that distorts risk scores
1.3 Determine if expedited audit is required Expedited audit commenced within 10 business days of trigger event; 0 High-risk providers left unaudited >6 months EASA/FAA safety bulletins arrive via email with no automated AMOS integration; manual triage by QA Manager creates response-time variability ⚠
2.1 Retrieve prior audit findings and open CARs CAR retrieval completed within 1 business day; zero open Level 1 CARs permitted before new audit commences Legacy AMOS implementations store CAR evidence as unstructured PDF attachments; keyword search across >500 CARs per provider is unreliable without structured data entry discipline
2.2 Generate compliance checklist from Part-145 requirements Checklist covers 100% of applicable regulatory subparts; prior repeat-finding items tagged for enhanced scrutiny EASA AMC amendments (e.g. 2022 Part-CAMO revision) require manual checklist updates; no automated regulatory change management feed into AMOS checklist library
2.3 Issue formal pre-audit notification to provider Notification issued ≥14 calendar days before audit start; document request acknowledgement received within 5 business days Some third-party MRO stations (particularly overseas line stations) acknowledge notifications informally via WhatsApp rather than through AMOS supplier portal, creating audit-trail gaps
2.4 Confirm provider readiness and document receipt 100% of requested documents received and reviewed before audit day 1; postponement rate <10% of scheduled audits AMOS document portal upload limits (50 MB per file) cause providers to split calibration certificate packages across multiple uploads, increasing pre-audit review time by 30–40% ⚠
3.1 Conduct opening meeting and confirm audit scope Opening meeting completed within 1 hour; scope agreed and documented before hangar/records access begins Remote line stations (e.g. outstation MRO) conduct opening meetings via video call; AMOS audit log does not capture video-session metadata, creating traceability gaps for regulatory inspectors
3.2 Audit technical records for airworthiness compliance AD compliance rate ≥99.5% across sampled aircraft tail numbers; zero unaddressed mandatory ADs permitted CAMP airworthiness data is not always synchronised with provider's AMOS work-order records in real time; delta between systems causes false AD non-compliance flags requiring manual reconciliation
3.3 Inspect maintenance facilities, tooling, and personnel Tooling calibration current for ≥99% of items sampled; zero expired Part-66 licences on active maintenance staff Calibration certificates managed in provider's local spreadsheet rather than AMOS; discrepancies between paper records and AMOS calibration register are a recurring Level 2 finding
3.4 Classify findings and identify Level 1 safety issues All findings classified within 4 hours of audit completion; Level 1 findings notified to Accountable Manager within 2 hours of identification EASA Level 1 / Level 2 classification criteria have grey areas (e.g. incomplete but not falsified records); inconsistent classification between auditors increases provider dispute rate and regulatory risk ⚠
3.5 Issue stop-work notice or immediate CAR for Level 1 Regulatory authority notification within 24 hours of Level 1 finding; stop-work enforced within 2 hours on affected task Stop-work authority at outstations requires on-call Accountable Manager approval; out-of-hours time-zone issues cause average 4-hour delay in Level 1 stop-work enforcement at overseas stations
4.1 Finalise audit report and issue CARs to provider Audit report issued within 5 business days; CAR numbering 100% traceable to audit report section AMOS CAR module does not auto-populate regulatory reference citations; auditors manually copy EASA AMC text, introducing transcription errors that providers use to dispute finding validity
4.2 Review and accept or reject provider CAP response CAP response received from provider within 10 business days (Level 1) / 30 days (Level 2); first-submission acceptance rate ≥75% Providers frequently submit '5-Why' root-cause analyses that misidentify systemic causes as individual errors, leading to repeat findings in subsequent audits; AMOS has no CAP quality scoring mechanism ⚠
4.3 Track CAP implementation and verify closure evidence CAR on-time closure rate ≥90%; zero Level 1 CARs open beyond 30 days; Level 2 CARs closed within 90 days AMOS CAR closure workflow requires Accountable Manager digital signature but does not enforce two-person review; single-reviewer closure of Level 1 CARs has been flagged in EASA SAFA audits as a systemic gap
5.1 Monitor fleet-wide AD and SB compliance status AD compliance rate ≥99.9% at fleet level; zero aircraft operated with overdue mandatory ADs; SB incorporation decisions documented within 30 days of issue CAMP receives FAA AD feeds automatically but EASA ADs require manual import from EASA e-Rules portal; 24–72 hour lag creates window where a newly effective EASA AD is not yet reflected in CAMP, risking inadvertent non-compliance
5.2 Analyse reliability programme outputs for adverse trends Reliability report published by 5th working day of each month; alert threshold breach investigated within 10 business days; IFSD rate ≤0.02 per 1,000 engine hours Delay/cancellation codes entered by operations staff often misclassify maintenance-caused events as ATC or weather; data quality issues in AMOS cause reliability metrics to undercount maintenance-driven disruptions ⚠
5.3 Submit mandatory occurrence reports to regulator 100% of reportable occurrences submitted within 72-hour regulatory deadline; MOR rejection rate <2%; follow-up investigation reports submitted within 30 days ECCAIRS 2 taxonomy (≥1,800 occurrence categories) is complex; front-line reporters default to generic codes, reducing data quality for safety trend analysis across the European aviation safety database
