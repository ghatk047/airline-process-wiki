StepL4 Step Name KPI / TargetPain Point / RiskGate
1.1 Identify MRO capability gap requiring outsourcing Vendor qualification cycle ≤90 days from gap identification AMOS capability mapping not always current; manual gap analysis against maintenance program is error-prone and reliant on engineering judgement rather than system-generated flags
1.2 Issue RFQ to approved vendor shortlist via Ariba RFQ response rate ≥80% of invited vendors within 14 days Limited pool of EASA Part-145 / FAA-approved vendors for narrowbody heavy checks creates market concentration risk and limits competitive pricing leverage
1.3 Evaluate vendor Part-145 approval and capability 100% of selected vendors hold current EASA Part-145 or FAA Part-145 approval at time of contract award Approval expiry tracking in AMOS requires manual certificate uploads; no automated feed from FAA AIRS or EASA EAMR — expired approvals may go undetected until audit ⚠
2.1 Define MRO work scope and SLA requirements Work scope completeness score ≥95% (no unplanned post-induction scope additions attributable to pre-induction planning gaps) Scope creep during heavy checks when undocumented corrosion or structural findings emerge; AMOS work orders not pre-linked to vendor contracts, making scope-change traceability manual
2.2 Negotiate contract terms, pricing, and TAT commitments C-check contract TAT ≤35 days; man-hour rate within ±5% of OAG/industry benchmark Negotiating fixed-price contracts with variable scope (structural findings, AD compliance work) leads to post-check cost disputes; penalty clauses rarely enforced due to vendor dependency ⚠
2.3 Load signed contract into SAP Ariba and AMOS Contract data entry accuracy 100%; dual-system load completed within 2 business days of signature Dual manual entry into SAP Ariba and AMOS creates synchronisation risk; contract amendments frequently update only one system, causing PO/work-order mismatches that surface at invoice reconciliation
3.1 Release aircraft to MRO vendor for scheduled induction On-time aircraft delivery to MRO station ≥95%; handover documentation completeness 100% Late aircraft delivery to MRO station (due to operational delays or AOG diversions) disrupts vendor slot allocation and triggers liquidated damages clauses; AMOS does not automatically flag operational conflicts with maintenance slot windows
3.2 Monitor in-shop progress against TAT milestones Milestone adherence ≥90%; TAT variance ≤±10% of contracted schedule at mid-check review Real-time visibility into vendor shop-floor status is absent in AMOS without a Resident Technical Representative on-site; no direct system-to-system integration between airline AMOS and most third-party MRO AMOS instances ⚠
3.3 Approve additional work findings (AWF) change orders AWF approval turnaround ≤4 hours to avoid vendor shop-floor downtime AMOS change order approval workflow requires multiple sign-off tiers; delays in AWF approval are a leading cause of TAT overrun in heavy maintenance — vendors charge idle-time fees while awaiting airline decisions ⚠
4.1 Collect vendor scorecard data after work completion On-time delivery ≥92%; rework rate ≤2%; defect escape rate (post-C-of-R finding) ≤0.5% Scorecard data is manually aggregated from AMOS work orders and SAP cost postings; no automated vendor KPI dashboard — quarterly scorecard preparation takes 3–5 days of analyst effort
4.2 Conduct formal quarterly vendor performance review Quarterly performance reviews completed 100% on schedule; SLA compliance rating reported within ±5% confidence interval Vendors frequently dispute scorecard data citing their own internal metrics; absence of shared system-to-system data exchange creates reconciliation disputes that delay corrective action by 30–60 days ⚠
4.3 Issue corrective action request (CAR) for SLA breach CAR vendor response rate 100% within 30 days; repeat findings reduced ≥50% within two subsequent work packages AMOS Quality module CAR tracking provides no vendor portal access; all CAR follow-up is conducted via email outside the system, creating an unauditable correspondence trail for EASA Part-CAMO findings
5.1 Review and validate MRO vendor invoices (3-way match) 3-way match success rate ≥98%; invoice dispute resolution ≤15 business days 3-way match between AMOS work order, SAP Ariba PO, and vendor invoice frequently fails because AWF change orders are not captured in the original PO — each failure requires manual re-match across two systems ⚠
5.2 Process vendor payment and post maintenance cost allocation Payment within contracted net-30 terms ≥95%; cost allocation accuracy 100% at aircraft registration level Aircraft cost centre mapping in SAP must align with AMOS aircraft registration codes; registration mismatches (e.g., wet-lease aircraft with swapped tails) cause fleet maintenance cost reporting errors that distort unit cost analysis
6.1 Evaluate contract renewal vs. re-tender decision Renewal or re-tender decision completed ≥90 days before contract expiry; benchmark pricing variance ≤8% from renewed rate SAP Ariba does not proactively alert on contract expiry approaching 90-day threshold; contracts default to auto-rollover at existing rates, forfeiting leverage — market benchmark data requires manual sourcing from OAG or industry surveys ⚠
6.2 Execute contract close-out or new-vendor transition Vendor transition completed with zero AOG events attributable to transition gap; transition plan signed off ≥60 days before go-live Transitioning open AMOS work orders from outgoing to incoming vendor is a manual process with no system-supported migration wizard; open AWF items and warranty claims risk being orphaned during handover
