Airline-Specific Risks & Pain Points

Manual monitoring of multiple regulatory feeds (FAA, EASA, TCCA) creates gaps when engineer is on leave; no automated ingestion in legacy CAMP configurations
FAA Emergency ADs may have 0-day compliance windows that conflict with active aircraft in revenue service, forcing AOG decisions without operational slack
Duplicate AD entries occur when engineers create records independently in AMOS and CAMP before integration sync runs, causing reconciliation rework
Config records in CAMP may lag behind actual installed mods if AMOS SB status is not synced in real time — risk of incorrect applicability determination
Mixed-fleet operators with multiple sub-variants (e.g. 737-700 vs 737-800) frequently have partial applicability — manual cross-check against Boeing TASK data required
AMOC approval from FAA typically takes 30–90 days; if AD compliance window is shorter, airline must comply with original method regardless of cost impact
